# PR_Power_Outages
Shaimar R. González Morales and Bejamin Huyhn
Johns Hopkins University Bloomberg School of Public Health, Department of Environmental Health and Engineering, Baltimore, MD
### Abstract
[!NOTE]
In 2017, Puerto Rico experienced one of the most devastating hurricanes in its history. To strengthen and quickly repair the energy grid, the government privatized the sector and, in 2020, signed a contract with a private energy corporation. Nevertheless, despite privatization, power outages have continued over the years. This analysis evaluates outages from the time this company assumed control of the grid through the most recent publicly available data. The findings show that outages not only persisted but worsened in reliability, particularly after another natural disaster, likely due to a lack of preparation and ongoing infrastructure deficiencies. Multilinear regression models assessing the effects of precipitation and temperature independently showed that only about 1% of the variation in power outages could be explained by weather conditions. This suggests that most outages were likely the result of mismanagement, poor maintenance, or structural failures due to the lack of oversight. Ultimately, the communities most affected continue to be the most vulnerable, particularly older adults, individuals with chronic illnesses, and residents of rural and underserved areas, who rely on uninterrupted electricity for their safety and health and may lack access to backup generators. Moving forward, government accountability is critical. The government should require measurable outcomes and regular public transparency reports. These steps, alongside investing in community-based solutions that support a transition to renewable energy, are essential to reduce disparities, protect vulnerable populations, and build a more resilient energy future for Puerto Rico.
